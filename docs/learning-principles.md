# Learning principles

The skills are built on a small number of findings that have held up, plus recent studies of AI tutors. This page says what the kit does because of each one, and how much weight the evidence can bear.

## What the kit does, and why

| The kit does this | Because |
|---|---|
| Asks for your attempt before explaining or marking | Retrieving an answer strengthens memory more than reading one (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006; Karpicke & Blunt, 2011) |
| Schedules reviews with growing gaps, capped by the time left before the exam | Spaced study beats massed study, and the best gap depends on how long you need to remember (Cepeda et al., 2006, 2008) |
| Mixes topics and courses within a session | Mixed practice is harder in the moment and better on a later test (Rohrer & Taylor, 2007; Kornell & Bjork, 2008) |
| Gives hints in steps and holds the full answer back | How much help to give is a real trade-off: too much and the learner does not think, too little and they stall (Koedinger & Aleven, 2007) |
| Has you write "In my own words" yourself | Material you generate is remembered better than material you read (Slamecka & Graf, 1978) |
| Has you name the connections before `brain-map` draws a concept map | Drawing a concept map from memory works as well as other retrieval practice, while drawing one with the text open does less (Karpicke & Blunt, 2011; Blunt & Karpicke, 2014) |
| Asks how confident you are before giving feedback | Learners misjudge what they know: the rereading group was the most confident and remembered least (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006) |
| Treats rereading and highlighting as a first pass only | A review of ten techniques rated both low, and practice testing and distributed practice high (Dunlosky et al., 2013) |
| Keeps the AI in a tutor role, grounded in your sources | See the next section |

These techniques share one property: they make studying feel slower and harder while it is happening. Bjork and Bjork (2011) call them desirable difficulties, and their point is that how well you perform during practice is an unreliable sign of how much you have learned.

## What studies of AI tutors show

The evidence is young and mixed. That is the reason for the kit's design, not a footnote to it.

- **Unrestricted AI help can hurt.** In a randomised trial with nearly 1,000 high-school students, a ChatGPT-style assistant raised scores during practice by 48 percent and lowered them by 17 percent on a later exam taken without help. A tutor version that gave hints and was grounded in teacher-written solutions raised practice scores by 127 percent and showed no harm on the exam, but no gain either. The students using the plain assistant did not notice that they had learned less (Bastani et al., 2025). One school, one subject, 2023 models.
- **A carefully designed tutor can work well.** In a crossover trial in a Harvard physics course, students learned more from an AI tutor built on stated teaching principles than from an active-learning class on the same material (Kestin et al., 2025). The test came straight after the lesson, the course was at a selective university, and the authors were the instructors.
- **Access is not learning.** In a university trial, a course chatbot that answered directly was followed by lower grades in part of the sample. Three out of four requests asked for information, explanations or solutions, and under one percent asked for feedback on the student's own work (Liu et al., 2026, a working paper that has not been peer reviewed).
- **How you use it matters.** Among developers learning a new library, those who delegated the work scored lower on a later quiz than those who asked conceptual questions (Shen & Tamkin, 2026, a small preprint).
- **Better products are not the same as better learning.** Students writing with ChatGPT produced better essays without gaining more knowledge than the comparison groups (Fan et al., 2025).

What the kit takes from this: the AI asks before it tells, stays close to your course material, says when your sources do not cover something, and leaves a record of what you could do without it.

## Limits

- Most of the classic studies measured recall of texts, facts and problem types over days or weeks. Effect sizes vary.
- The AI studies are few, short, and tied to particular tools and settings. The picture will change.
- No technique replaces understanding the material in the first place. Testing yourself on something you never understood is guessing.
