# Retrieval practice

## In my own words

Write this yourself after reading the source: two to six sentences, without looking.

## Key points

- Recalling from memory strengthens memory more than rereading does. [[how-learning-works#Retrieval practice]]
- Rereading wins after five minutes and loses after a week. [[how-learning-works#Retrieval practice]]
- Confidence after rereading is a poor guide to what will be remembered. [[how-learning-works#Retrieval practice]]

## Self-test

> [!question]- A student rereads a chapter four times and feels well prepared. Why might they still do worse a week later than a student who read it once and tested themselves?
> Retrieval strengthens memory more than rereading. In Roediger and Karpicke's experiment the rereading group led after five minutes (83 against 71 percent) and trailed after a week (40 against 61 percent). Rereading builds familiarity, which feels like knowing. [[how-learning-works#Retrieval practice]]

> [!question]- What did the concept-mapping study add to the case for retrieval practice?
> Retrieval practice beat an active, elaborate technique, 67 against 45 percent a week later, and it won even when the final test was to draw a concept map. The students had predicted the opposite. [[how-learning-works#Retrieval practice]]

> [!question]- Name three things the research suggests you do differently when studying.
> Test yourself before you feel ready, recall with the text closed, and do not trust the feeling of knowing that rereading gives. [[how-learning-works#Retrieval practice]]
