# Interleaving

## In my own words

Write this yourself after reading the source: two to six sentences, without looking.

## Key points

- Mixing problem types lowers performance during practice and raises it on a later test. [[how-learning-works#Interleaving]]
- Learners tend to judge blocked practice as better even when mixed practice served them better. [[how-learning-works#Interleaving]]
- Testing, spacing and interleaving are all desirable difficulties: harder now, better later. [[how-learning-works#Desirable difficulties]]

## Self-test

> [!question]- A classmate says mixed practice is inefficient because they get more wrong. What does the evidence say?
> More errors during practice is the expected pattern. In the mathematics study the mixed group scored 60 percent during practice against 89 for the blocked group, and 63 against 20 a week later. Performance while practising is a poor measure of learning. The sample was small, about 18 students. [[how-learning-works#Interleaving]]

> [!question]- Why might mixing help on an exam in particular?
> A likely reason is that mixed practice makes you choose which method a problem needs, which is what an exam asks. Blocked practice lets you skip that choice. [[how-learning-works#Interleaving]]

> [!question]- When is a difficulty not a desirable one?
> When the learner lacks the background to respond to it. Testing yourself on material you never understood is guessing, not practice. [[how-learning-works#Desirable difficulties]]
